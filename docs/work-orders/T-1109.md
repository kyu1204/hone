# T-1109 A cheaper candidate that is as good is the winner

## Goal
Found in T-1103. The winner is the candidate with the highest pass rate on the dev cases, whatever it costs. On the helpdesk agent the model's prompt scored 90% on the small model and 92% with the larger one at 4.9 times the cost per case (98% and 100% in the other framework). One trial of difference out of 48 decided for the expensive one, and that is what went to the held-out check. No one who pays the bill would choose that.

## Design
- Searching is unchanged: the next change is built on the candidate with the highest pass rate.
- Choosing the winner, once the search is over: take the candidate with the highest pass rate. If another candidate costs clearly less (under 90% of its cost on the dev cases) and is as good, the cheapest such candidate is the winner instead.
- "As good" is the one-standard-error rule of model selection (Breiman et al. 1984): take the simplest model whose score is within one standard error of the best. Here, over the dev cases, the mean difference between the two candidates case by case is no larger than the standard error of that difference. With pass rates per case that comes to: they differ by one case, or their differences cancel.
- The baseline is a candidate in this: a change that costs clearly more and gains one case is no gain.
- The account says what was passed over and why, with both pass rates and the cost ratio. The ledger records it as `passedOver`.
